## Sample materials

**Heating carbon dioxide** (Calculus II challenge problem). Students get NIST's reference data for the heat capacity of CO₂ and a story about a heater whose meter may be broken. They build a model, integrate it, and decide whether to report the meter, then compare their model with NIST's own fit and with a physicist's model built from molecular vibrations. Hints are printed upside down at the bottom of each page. [Problem (PDF)](/files/teaching/heating_co2.pdf) · [Data (Excel)](/files/teaching/co2_heat_capacity.xlsx)

**The horizon** (Calculus II written homework). Surveyors' rule of thumb says the curve of the Earth hides about 8 inches at 1 mile, growing with the square of the distance. Students derive that rule from a Taylor series, then find how far away the horizon is when you stand up. [Homework (PDF)](/files/teaching/horizon_homework.pdf) · [Interactive simulation](/files/horizon.html)

**Slicing as $Q = f \cdot m$** (Calculus II lecture redesign). Density, population, and work problems all share one idea: a total that is a product, where one factor is held fixed on each slice and the other is added up. I wrote notes that teach these units from that single idea. [Notes (PDF)](/files/teaching/slicing_notes.pdf)

**An infinite wedding cake** (Calculus II written homework). A client insists that a cake with infinitely many layers has finite volume. The client is right, but the cake would need infinitely much icing. [Homework (PDF)](/files/teaching/wedding_cake.pdf)

**Applied derivative problems** (Elements of Calculus, online). A rollercoaster mechanic tracks down a rattle in the track, and an investor answers an audit question, each with one derivative. [Problems (PDF)](/files/teaching/derivative_word_problems.pdf)
