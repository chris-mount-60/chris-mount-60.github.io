I am a Ph.D. candidate in Mathematics at the University of Arizona, advised by Ibrahim Fatkullin, and I expect to finish in May 2027.

## Research

I work in probability and stochastic processes. My research is on diffusions with sticky and reflecting boundary behavior, interacting particle systems with sticky interactions, Brownian local time and Ray–Knight theory, and exact simulation and Monte Carlo methods for diffusions. My dissertation (working title: *Sticky Brownian Motion: Exact Bridges and Two-Particle Dynamics with Discontinuous Diffusivity*) studies exact bridges of sticky Brownian motion and the dynamics of two particles that stick to each other.

## Teaching

I have taught mathematics since 2017, at Jacksonville State University and the University of Arizona, in courses from developmental mathematics and college algebra to statistics and calculus. I like to build courses around real questions: a problem that starts with a faulty meter or a ship disappearing over the horizon, and ends with the calculus that answers it. My [teaching page](/teaching/) has the courses I have taught and samples of materials I have written.
