Teaching

Heating carbon dioxide

Calculus II challenge problem

Students get NIST's reference data for the heat capacity of CO₂ and a story about a heater whose meter may be broken. They build a model, integrate it, and decide whether to report the meter, then compare their model with NIST's own fit and with a physicist's model built from molecular vibrations. Hints are printed upside down at the bottom of each page.

The horizon

Calculus II written homework

Surveyors' rule of thumb says the curve of the Earth hides about 8 inches at 1 mile, growing with the square of the distance. Students derive that rule from a Taylor series, then use the series coefficients to find derivatives at zero. Interactive simulation

Slicing as Q = f · m

Calculus II lecture redesign

Density, population, and work problems all share one idea: a total that is a product, where one factor is held fixed on each slice and the other is added up. I wrote notes that teach these units from that single idea.

An infinite wedding cake

Calculus II written homework

A client insists that a cake with infinitely many layers has finite volume. The client is right, but the cake would need infinitely much icing.
